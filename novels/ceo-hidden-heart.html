The CEO's Hidden Heart

Chapter One: The Man Everyone Feared

Chapter One

The Man Everyone Feared

The first thing Ava Bennett learned about Alexander Hayes was that nobody looked him in the eyes.

Not his employees.

Not his business partners.

And certainly not the people who had made the mistake of disappointing him.

Ava discovered this on her first morning at Hayes International.

She stood inside the enormous glass building with a folder pressed tightly against her chest, staring at the elevator numbers as they climbed toward the thirty-second floor.

Her palms were sweating.

She wiped them against her skirt and took a slow breath.

“You can do this,” she whispered.

The elevator doors opened.

Ava stepped inside.

A few employees followed her, but the moment someone mentioned the CEO's name, the entire elevator became silent.

“Mr. Hayes is already here,” a woman whispered.

Another employee immediately checked her phone.

“Then I hope he doesn't call the department today.”

Ava glanced at them.

“Is he really that bad?”

Everyone looked at her.

The woman beside her lowered her voice.

“You're new, aren't you?”

Ava nodded.

“First day.”

The woman gave her a sympathetic smile.

“Then nobody has warned you.”

“Warned me about what?”

The woman leaned closer.

“Alexander Hayes.”

Before Ava could answer, the elevator doors opened.

Everyone walked out.

The woman stopped briefly.

“One piece of advice.”

Ava waited.

“Don't give him a reason to remember your name.”

Then she walked away.

Ava stared after her.

“Well,” she whispered.

“That was encouraging.”

The Interview

Two weeks earlier, Ava had never imagined that she would be standing inside one of the largest companies in the city.

She had applied for the position of executive assistant after seeing the advertisement online.

The salary was more than she had expected.

The position was permanent.

And the company had an excellent reputation.

There was only one problem.

The position was directly under the CEO.

During the interview, the human resources manager had warned her.

“The CEO has very high standards.”

Ava had smiled.

“So do I.”

The woman had looked surprised.

“You're not afraid of him?”

Ava had laughed.

“I've never met him.”

“That will change.”

It had changed sooner than she expected.

The office doors opened automatically as Ava approached.

She walked toward the reception desk.

“Good morning.”

The receptionist looked up.

“You must be Ava.”

“Yes.”

“Mr. Hayes is expecting you.”

Ava's stomach tightened.

“Already?”

The receptionist smiled.

“His office is at the end of the hallway.”

Ava thanked her.

She walked down the hallway.

Every step made her more nervous.

Finally, she reached the large black door.

She raised her hand.

Knock.

Nothing.

She knocked again.

“Come in.”

His voice was deep.

Calm.

Almost frighteningly calm.

Ava opened the door.

And for the first time, she saw Alexander Hayes.

Alexander Hayes

He was standing beside the enormous windows overlooking the city.

Tall.

Broad-shouldered.

Dressed in a perfectly fitted black suit.

His dark hair was neatly styled, and his expression was impossible to read.

He turned slowly.

His eyes met hers.

Ava suddenly understood why nobody looked at him for too long.

His eyes were cold.

Not angry.

Just cold.

Like he had built a wall around himself and had no intention of letting anyone through it.

“Ava Bennett?”

“Yes.”

“You're late.”

Ava glanced at the clock.

“It's eight fifty-nine.”

“Your contract says eight fifty-five.”

She blinked.

“So I'm four minutes late.”

His eyebrow lifted slightly.

“You're correcting me?”

“No.”

“It sounded like it.”

Ava took a breath.

“I'm sorry.”

He stared at her for several seconds.

Then he looked down at the folder in his hand.

“Your qualifications are impressive.”

“Thank you.”

“Your previous employer described you as stubborn.”

Ava's eyes widened.

“They said that?”

“They did.”

“I prefer determined.”

For the first time, something almost resembling amusement appeared in his eyes.

It disappeared immediately.

“We'll see.”

He handed her a file.

“Your first assignment.”

Ava accepted it.

“What is it?”

“A presentation for the board meeting.”

She opened the folder.

“The meeting is today.”

“Yes.”

Ava stared at him.

“And you want me to prepare this today?”

“You applied to work for me.”

“Yes.”

“Then work.”

Ava looked at the enormous stack of documents.

Then she looked at him.

“Anything else?”

Alexander's expression remained unreadable.

“Don't disappoint me.”

The First Challenge

By ten thirty in the morning, Ava had already gone through three cups of coffee.

Her desk was covered with papers.

Numbers.

Reports.

Financial statements.

Business proposals.

Everything had to be organized before the meeting.

She glanced toward Alexander's office.

The glass doors were closed.

He was inside, working.

She wondered how someone could look so calm while making another person feel like they were about to have a nervous breakdown.

At eleven fifteen, her computer suddenly froze.

Ava stared at the screen.

“No.”

She clicked the mouse.

Nothing.

“Please don't do this to me.”

She tried again.

The screen remained frozen.

Ava closed her eyes.

“Okay. Stay calm.”

A shadow appeared beside her desk.

“Problem?”

Ava opened her eyes.

Alexander was standing beside her.

“My computer froze.”

He looked at the screen.

“How much have you saved?”

Ava froze.

“I haven't.”

Silence.

Alexander slowly looked at her.

“You haven't saved your work?”

“I was going to.”

He closed his eyes for a moment.

“You were going to.”

Ava nodded.

“Yes.”

He turned toward her.

“Do you know how much time you've wasted?”

Ava stood up.

“I can fix it.”

“How?”

She pointed at the computer.

“Give me five minutes.”

“You have three.”

He walked away.

Ava stared at him.

“Three minutes?”

She immediately got to work.

The Board Meeting

At exactly two o'clock, the boardroom doors opened.

Executives walked inside.

Ava stood beside Alexander with the presentation ready on the screen.

Her heart was pounding.

Alexander looked at her.

“Relax.”

Ava looked at him in surprise.

“You know how to say that?”

He almost smiled.

Almost.

“Don't make me regret it.”

The meeting began.

Everything went smoothly for the first twenty minutes.

Then one of the directors raised his hand.

“Mr. Hayes, there's a problem with the third-quarter projection.”

Alexander looked at the screen.

“What problem?”

“The numbers don't match the report we received last month.”

Ava looked at the document.

She noticed something.

The figures were wrong.

Someone had changed them.

She leaned toward Alexander.

“May I speak?”

He looked at her.

“Go ahead.”

Ava stood.

“The projection isn't wrong.”

Everyone looked at her.

She continued.

“The report we received last month was altered.”

The room became silent.

One of the directors frowned.

“That's a serious accusation.”

Ava nodded.

“I'm aware.”

She pointed toward the screen.

“But the original numbers are stored in the company's previous financial records.”

Alexander stared at her.

“Are you certain?”

“Yes.”

He looked at the director.

“We'll verify it.”

The meeting continued.

But Ava could feel Alexander watching her.

After the Meeting

The moment everyone left the boardroom, Ava gathered the documents.

“Ava.”

She stopped.

Alexander was standing behind her.

“Yes?”

“Where did you learn to notice financial discrepancies like that?”

Ava hesitated.

“My father.”

His expression changed slightly.

“What did he do?”

“He was an accountant.”

“Was?”

Ava looked away.

“He passed away.”

Alexander became silent.

“I'm sorry.”

Ava looked at him.

It was the first time his voice sounded genuinely gentle.

“Thank you.”

She turned to leave.

“Ava.”

She turned around.

“Yes?”

“Good work today.”

She smiled.

“Was that a compliment?”

Alexander's lips curved slightly.

“Don't get used to it.”

The Secret

That evening, Ava was packing her things when she heard voices coming from Alexander's office.

She stopped.

The door was slightly open.

She hadn't intended to listen.

But then she heard her name.

“Ava Bennett.”

Ava froze.

Another man's voice answered.

“You're certain she's the one?”

Alexander's voice was low.

“I'm certain.”

Ava's heart began beating faster.

The other man spoke again.

“Then what are you going to do?”

Silence.

Then Alexander said something that made Ava's blood run cold.

“I'm going to find out why she came here.”

Ava stepped backward.

Her handbag slipped from her shoulder.

It hit the floor.

The sound echoed through the hallway.

Silence followed.

Ava quickly picked up her bag.

She turned toward the elevator.

The office door opened behind her.

“Ava.”

She stopped.

Slowly, she turned around.

Alexander stood in the doorway.

His expression had completely changed.

There was no coldness now.

There was something else.

Fear.

“Did you hear something?”

Ava swallowed.

“No.”

He stared at her.

“Are you sure?”

“Yes.”

He walked toward her.

One step.

Then another.

Ava stood completely still.

Alexander stopped in front of her.

“You should go home.”

“I was already leaving.”

“Good.”

Ava walked toward the elevator.

Just before the doors closed, she looked back.

Alexander was still standing there.

Watching her.

His expression was impossible to understand.

But there was something in his eyes.

Something that made Ava wonder if the terrifying CEO everyone feared was hiding something much deeper.

The Photograph

Alexander waited until the elevator disappeared.

Then he returned to his office.

His brother, Daniel, was standing beside the desk.

“She heard us,” Daniel said.

Alexander loosened his tie.

“I know.”

Daniel placed an old photograph on the desk.

Alexander stared at it.

The photograph showed a young woman standing beside a little girl.

The little girl couldn't have been more than six years old.

Alexander picked up the photograph.

“She looks exactly like her mother.”

Daniel nodded.

“That's why I told you not to hire her.”

Alexander looked at the photograph again.

“I didn't know it was her.”

“And now?”

Alexander's jaw tightened.

“Now I need the truth.”

Daniel stared at him.

“What if she finds out what happened fifteen years ago?”

Alexander looked toward the window.

The city lights reflected against the glass.

“She will.”

“And when she does?”

Alexander remained silent.

Then he whispered,

“I'll tell her everything.”

The Message

Across the city, Ava entered her apartment and dropped her handbag onto the sofa.

She couldn't stop thinking about Alexander.

About the way he had looked at her.

About the conversation.

About the strange photograph she hadn't seen.

She walked into her bedroom.

Her phone buzzed.

Unknown Number.

Ava frowned.

She opened the message.

There was only one sentence.

“Stay away from Alexander Hayes if you want to know the truth about your father.”

Ava's hands went cold.

She read the message again.

Her father.

How could a stranger know about him?

Another message appeared.

“He has been lying to you.”

Ava's heart pounded.

She looked toward the window.

The curtains moved slightly in the night breeze.

For the first time since starting her new job, Ava realized something.

She hadn't accidentally walked into Alexander Hayes' world.

Someone had brought her there.

And Alexander Hayes knew exactly why.

Ava stared at her phone.

Then another message appeared.

“Ask him about the night your father died.”

Ava stopped breathing.

Her father had died fifteen years ago.

And as far as she knew, Alexander Hayes had never met him.

Or had he?

End of Chapter One
